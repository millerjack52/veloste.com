Festival Gallery Hop

1–9 November 2025

Thirteen stops across Banff during the 50th anniversary Banff Mountain Film & Book Festival. New exhibitions, murals, artist talks, guided tours and a bakery with art on its walls.

Self-guided and free to wander. Most stops are a short walk from Bear Street; the Banff Centre, Cave and Basin, the Juniper and the Fairmont Banff Springs are a short drive or bus ride away.

Where
Banff Centre for Arts and Creativity, 107 Tunnel Mountain Drive

Fifty years of the festival. An international competition and annual presentation of short films, books and documentaries about mountain culture, sports and the environment, hosted at the Banff Centre every fall.

Festival programme
When
Opening November 1
Where
Whyte Museum of the Canadian Rockies, 111 Bear Street

Three interconnected bodies of work that resonate deeply in Banff, where mountain culture converges with narratives of conquest, grandeur and nostalgia. Through rigorous research and lens-based media, Rasmussen recasts traditional narratives of history and nature through an exploration of the French and Swiss Alps.

"Did You Know Blue Had No Name?" interrogates the symbolism of blueness, from Saussure's cyanometer to the first ascent of Mont Blanc. "The Year Without a Summer" links an 1816 climate anomaly to today's environmental crises. "Nostalgia: A Return to the Alps in Five Vignettes" examines the Alps as symbols of healing and escape.

Organized by The Whyte and curated by Christina Cuthbertson

In attendance Saturday, November 8, 12 to 4 pm
Where
Canada House Gallery, 201 Bear Street, corner of Bear and Caribou

Richard Cole explores how light moves through the landscape: how it softens distance, reveals quiet beauty and brings a sense of renewal to familiar places. Created in a time of uncertainty, the collection reflects on moments when hope breaks through, a clearing sky, a glow after rain, the stillness that follows a passing storm.

A reflection on resilience and light, and a quiet reminder to pause, breathe and notice the strength that exists within the landscape and within ourselves.

Richard Cole, Memory of the Sisters, oil on canvas

Wild Flour Artisan Bakery, 211 Bear Street #101

Wild Flour has long celebrated local artists, showcasing rotating exhibitions year-round on its walls. This month, Ron Hallam presents striking images of the Canadian Rockies, capturing moments of light and perspective that reveal the beauty and wonder of the park. Enjoy fresh-baked treats while experiencing the Rockies through Ron's lens.

Where
Bison Courtyard, 211 Bear Street

Four large-scale murals by renowned Indigenous artist Joseph M. Sánchez, a founding member of the Professional Native Indian Artists Inc., also known as the Indigenous Group of Seven. Commissioned for the Courtyard's 20th anniversary, the murals explore Indigenous history, spirituality and humanity's connection to the natural world, with the bison symbolizing hope for future generations.

About the artist
When
October 22, 2025 to January 7, 2026
Where
Walter Phillips Gallery, Banff Centre, 107 Tunnel Mountain Drive

Over four summers on Newfoundland's Fogo Island, Sharon Lockhart developed a new film and a series of photographs, building on the themes that define her career: an exploration of place and how people engage with landscapes, a durational approach to attention through extended static takes, and a deep commitment to those who appear before her lens.

The exhibition features her film installation WINDWARD, in which the island's striking geology, unique climate and austere beauty are brought to life through portrayals of youth, and debuts the photographs Fogo Island Portrait Studio.

Sharon Lockhart, still from WINDWARD, 2025. Co-commissioned by Shorefast/Fogo Island Arts, The Vega Foundation and the National Gallery of Canada. Courtesy the artist and neugerriemschneider, Berlin. © Sharon Lockhart, 2025

A quick introduction to all the highlights Cave and Basin has to offer. This accessible 30-minute indoor and outdoor tour of the 1914 Bathing Pavilion covers Indigenous significance, historic architecture, a mysterious cave, the beginnings of national parks in Canada, and the tiny, endangered Banff Springs Snail.

Free with admission or Discovery Pass

Where
The Juniper Hotel & Bistro, 1 Juniper Way

The Juniper proudly showcases original works and cultural artifacts by Indigenous artists from coast to coast to coast, including pieces by all seven members of the Indigenous Group of Seven and Inuit artists from the Arctic. The collection celebrates Indigenous culture, storytelling and connection to the land, enjoyed alongside panoramic views of the Bow Valley.

J. Kakegamic, Animals In Conflict

About the collection
Where
Mountain Galleries at the Fairmont Banff Springs, 405 Spray Avenue

One of Canada's leading contemporary art galleries. Since 1992, Mountain Galleries has showcased a curated selection of paintings, sculpture, photography and ceramics from established and emerging Canadian artists, reflecting the landscapes and stories of the Canadian wilderness.

The Carter-Ryan Gallery, 229 Bear Street, corner of Bear and Wolf

Celebrated Cree artist Jason Carter will be in the Banff gallery to talk about being an artist "outside the lens". Once a prolific camera operator in morning television, Jason now works through a different, more cinematic lens. His mediums are stone and canvas, but his point of view has remained the same.
