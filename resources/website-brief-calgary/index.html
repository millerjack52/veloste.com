Start with decisions, not decoration

The brief is the first design tool.

“We need a modern website” describes a feeling, but it does not define the work. A useful project brief explains what needs to change for the business and for the people using the site. It gives a potential web partner enough context to challenge weak assumptions and recommend an appropriate scope.

This template reflects the questions Veloste uses to begin website discovery. You do not need perfect answers. Unknowns are useful when they are made visible early, because they can be resolved before they become late-stage changes to design, content, integrations, or launch timing.

How to write a useful website brief

Five sections that prevent expensive ambiguity.

  1. 01

    Name the business change

    Separate the requested output from the desired result. “Build a five-page website” is an output. “Help commercial property managers understand our maintenance program and request a site assessment” explains the change the website should support. The second statement gives strategy, content, design, and measurement a common direction.

  2. 02

    Choose a primary audience and action

    A website may serve several audiences, but every page cannot prioritize all of them equally. Identify the customer whose decision matters most, what they need to understand, and the next action that represents progress. Secondary audiences can still have clear routes without weakening the primary journey.

  3. 03

    Distinguish requirements from ideas

    A payment system required to sell online is a requirement. A homepage animation may be a useful idea, but its value depends on the message, audience, performance constraints, and budget. Labelling each clearly lets the web developer protect essential work while testing optional ideas against the project goal.

  4. 04

    Audit content before estimating delivery

    Page count alone does not describe content effort. Confirm who will write, review, and approve the copy; whether photography or diagrams exist; which claims have evidence; and whether legal or technical reviewers are involved. Content is often the dependency that determines whether a launch date is realistic.

  5. 05

    Define success in observable terms

    “Looks professional” matters, but it is difficult to measure. Add outcomes such as qualified form submissions, calls from a priority service area, completed purchases, applications, booking completions, or fewer support questions. A credible measurement plan also identifies which analytics and business systems can verify those actions.

Using the brief in Calgary

Compare recommendations, not just totals.

Give the same brief to each Calgary web developer you are seriously considering. A useful response should explain the recommended scope, important assumptions, responsibilities, exclusions, review stages, launch approach, ownership, and post-launch support. If two proposals differ substantially, compare what each includes before treating the totals as equivalent.

You should also expect a good partner to ask follow-up questions. The brief is not a substitute for discovery; it makes discovery more productive. If every requirement is accepted without discussion, important trade-offs may simply have been deferred until after the proposal.
